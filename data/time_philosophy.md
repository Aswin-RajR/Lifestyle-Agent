# Human Time Management Rules

> **Pillar:** Purpose & Time
> **Problem it solves:** Mindless phone scrolling, hours wasted on low-value tasks, and a lack of clear, meaningful goals.

## Core Philosophy

Time is the one resource that cannot be earned back. Most people don't lose time in big dramatic chunks — they lose it in small, invisible leaks: a few minutes here, an hour of scrolling there. The goal of this pillar is not to create a rigid, joyless schedule, but to protect a person's *attention* so it can be spent on what actually matters to them.

---

## 1. The Eat-the-Frog Rule

**Rule:** Do the most meaningful or hardest task first thing in the morning, before checking email, social media, or news.

**Why it works:**
- Willpower and mental energy are highest early in the day and decline with use (a concept often called "decision fatigue" or "ego depletion").
- Completing the hardest task first removes the psychological weight of dread that builds up when it's postponed.
- It prevents "productive procrastination" — doing easy, low-value tasks to feel busy while avoiding the one task that actually matters.

**How the app should apply this:**
- Each morning, prompt the user to name **one "Frog"** — the single task they'd most like to avoid but know matters most.
- Block the first 60–90 minutes of the user's day for this task, before any notifications or scrolling is allowed.
- Track "Frog Completion Streaks" rather than total tasks completed, since consistency matters more than volume.

---

## 2. The 7-8-9 Rule

**Rule:** A balanced day is built on three numbers:
- **7 hours of sleep**
- **8 hours of focused work**
- **9 hours for family, self-care, and personal growth**

**Why it works:**
- This rule mirrors the long-standing "8-8-8" balance concept (8 hours work, 8 hours rest, 8 hours leisure), adjusted to prioritize sleep quality and personal connection over raw leisure time.
- Sleep is treated as a non-negotiable foundation, not a flexible buffer — since cutting sleep to gain "extra hours" almost always backfires through lower focus and worse decisions the next day.
- Splitting the remaining hours into clearly labeled categories (work vs. people-and-self) prevents work from silently expanding to fill all available time, which is one of the most common causes of burnout.

**How the app should apply this:**
- Visualize the user's day as three colored blocks (Sleep / Work / Life) that should roughly add up to 24 hours.
- Send a gentle alert if "Work" hours regularly exceed 8–9 hours while "Life" hours shrink below 6.
- Allow flexibility — this is a *guideline ratio*, not a rigid clock-in/clock-out system. The app should ask "Did today feel balanced?" rather than judge by the numbers alone.

---

## Guiding Principle for the App

> **Protect attention before protecting time.**
> A calendar full of blocked hours means nothing if the mind is still half-scrolling a phone. Every feature under this pillar should ask: *"Does this help the person notice where their attention is going, and choose it on purpose?"*
