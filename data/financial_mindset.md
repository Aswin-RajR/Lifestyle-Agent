# Holistic Wealth Rules

> **Pillar:** Financial Health
> **Problem it solves:** Impulsive buying habits and a lack of structured, sustainable saving behavior.

## Core Philosophy

Most financial stress doesn't come from a single bad decision — it comes from hundreds of small, unexamined ones. The goal of this pillar isn't aggressive investing advice or get-rich strategies; it's building *slow, boring, repeatable habits* that quietly compound over time. Holistic wealth means having enough breathing room that money stops being a constant source of anxiety.

---

## 1. The 24-Hour Cooling Rule

**Rule:** Wait 24 hours before spending money on any non-essential item over $50.

**Why it works:**
- Impulse purchases are driven by short bursts of emotional reward (excitement, novelty, social validation) that fade quickly — often within a day.
- Introducing a mandatory delay separates the *emotional urge* to buy from the *actual decision* to buy, giving the rational part of the brain time to catch up.
- This is a well-documented behavioral economics technique: friction reduces impulsive action far more effectively than willpower alone.

**How the app should apply this:**
- When the user logs a planned purchase over $50, start a visible 24-hour countdown timer instead of letting them mark it "bought" immediately.
- After 24 hours, re-ask: *"Do you still want this?"* — and log the answer, building a personal history of how often delayed wants turn into "no."
- Celebrate the *skipped* purchases as wins, not just the saved purchases — reframe restraint as a positive action, not a deprivation.

---

## 2. Micro-Savings Goal

**Rule:** Save a small, fixed percentage of income (even just 5%) at the very start of every week, before any other spending happens.

**Why it works:**
- This follows the "pay yourself first" principle: saving becomes automatic and protected rather than whatever happens to be left over after spending.
- Small percentages feel psychologically painless, which makes the habit sustainable for months and years rather than abandoned after a few weeks.
- Consistency matters more than amount — a 5% saved every single week builds both a financial cushion and a stronger savings *identity* ("I am someone who saves") over time.

**How the app should apply this:**
- Prompt the user at the start of each week (e.g., Sunday evening or Monday morning) to set aside their chosen percentage before tracking any other spending.
- Allow the percentage to be adjusted, but never let it default to 0% — always nudge toward at least a token amount.
- Show a simple, encouraging running total ("You've protected $X over 8 weeks") rather than complex charts or comparisons to others.

---

## Guiding Principle for the App

> **Make the safe choice the default, not the difficult one.**
> The app should never lecture the user about discipline. Instead, it should quietly restructure the *path of least resistance* so that saving and pausing before spending feel like the natural, easy option — not a constant uphill fight against temptation.
